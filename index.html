Dynamic Credit Limit Optimization

Can reinforcement learning allocate credit more effectively under a shared risk budget?

I built an end-to-end research pipeline combining a longitudinal customer simulator, a calibrated probability-of-default model and PPO policies. Each monthly credit-limit decision changes future borrowing, losses and the capacity available to the rest of the portfolio.

From risk prediction to sequential decisions

A frozen 12-month default model feeds observable customer and portfolio features into the allocation policy. Credit-limit requests range from −20% to +20%; a shared admission layer checks expected-loss, exposure and concentration ceilings. Learned policies are compared with static, risk-based, greedy and integer-optimization baselines on matched stochastic scenarios.

Measured results

Normal macro, medium risk budget (€150 monthly expected-loss allowance per initial customer), up to 24 months. Main comparisons use 24 paired portfolios of 12 customers; PPO results aggregate three training seeds.

What the evidence shows

PPO improves mean value over Myopic in the nominal case, but its +€83.16 gain over fixed contraction is inconclusive (95% CI [−€20.04, +€235.12]). It also produces 7.06 percentage points more defaults than Myopic despite lower losses.

Under stress, PPO reaches −€1,022.9 per customer versus −€849.9 for fixed contraction. Hard admission prevents new immediate breaches or worsening inherited ones; it does not eliminate deficits caused by existing debt or changing risk. These are synthetic research results, not evidence of real-world banking performance.
