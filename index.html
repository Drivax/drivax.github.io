Dynamic Credit Limit Optimization

What does reinforcement learning actually learn when optimizing repeated credit-limit decisions?

I built a reproducible research pipeline combining a synthetic longitudinal customer simulator, calibrated default prediction and PPO. Monthly limit changes affect payment incentives, purchasing capacity, future balances and default exposure. The study compares learned policies with transparent rules and an economic baseline, including a constant-contraction control to test whether learning adds value.

From risk prediction to sequential decisions

A frozen, calibrated logistic model estimates 12-month default probability from observable histories (test ROC-AUC 0.842; Brier score 0.162). PPO observes 21 customer and macro features and chooses monthly limit changes from −20%, −10%, unchanged, +10% or +20%. Limits stay within €500–€15,000; three consecutive delinquent months block increases. Hidden customer traits and future shocks are never policy inputs.

Reproducible experimental design

The canonical experiment follows 300 held-out customers for up to 24 months, with absorbing default. All policies share initial customers and indexed exogenous shocks. Each of three PPO seeds trains for 32,768 transitions, with checkpoints selected on a separate validation population. Baseline macro is normal throughout; severe stress applies in months 4–19 at severity 1.6. Confidence intervals use 300 crossed bootstrap resamples of customers and PPO seeds.

Key finding: contraction, not demonstrated learned planning

Every PPO seed reproduces the effective trajectories of AlwaysDecrease20 to numerical precision on this evaluation panel. The experiment supports exposure contraction under the specified objective, but demonstrates no advantage over the constant −20% rule.

Lower monetary losses do not imply fewer defaults: PPO default incidence exceeds MyopicEconomic by 14.33 percentage points at baseline and 8.00 points under stress. Neither terminal receivables nor customer welfare are valued. The simulator is fully synthetic and is not calibrated to a real bank; these results do not establish deployment performance.

Study scope: portfolio allocation, robustness and off-policy evaluation are supplementary studies with different protocols. Their figures are kept separate from the individual-customer results shown here.
